## 5. ABM: outreach to a named company list
The customer is countable by name: 1,614 AI devices in the FDA register, ~28 added per month, while Google purchase searches are few. Previous outbound got a 2% reply rate, without a prepared list, a buying signal or a recognisable sender. Those three things change.

### 5.1 Company list and decision-makers
- **Sources:** FDA AI device register (companies with 1–10 devices), seed-to-Series B medtech AI rounds, HLTH attendee/exhibitor list, large medtechs with digital and ML teams.
- **Segments:** A (post-FDA), B (pre-FDA, post-round), C (corporate department). Start with A and B: fastest decisions.
- **People per company (2–3):** CTO, VP Engineering, Head of AI/ML, Head of Data; regulatory lead at pre-FDA companies. ~21,000 such people on LinkedIn in D1 US industries.
- **Buying signals:** new FDA device, new round, new CTO or Head of AI, ML/MLOps job ad, new model version or PCCP. Companies with a signal jump the queue.
- **Where:** Pipedrive, each company tagged with segment, signal and date.

### 5.2 Sequences (3–4 steps over ~3 weeks, one concrete reason to talk)
| Sequence | For | Message | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-FDA | Segment B | data and model pipeline ready for FDA submission and PCCP before it becomes a problem | relevant case study, PCCP guide |
| Post-FDA | Segment A | maintain and ship new model versions without resubmission: MLOps for PCCP, monitoring, audit | relevant case studies |
| Post-signal | any company with a new signal | short note referencing the event and the one problem that usually follows | closest case study |

Step 1: LinkedIn invite from the CEO, no pitch · Step 2: one concrete point (problem, number, short case) · Step 3: a resource (PCCP or QMSR article) plus a 20-minute call offer · Step 4: short reminder; no reply = back to the list until the next signal.

### 5.3 HLTH as part of ABM
HLTH (15–18 Nov, Las Vegas): 12,250+ attendees (4,400+ C-suite), 950+ exhibitors, 8,500+ meetings. Pull segment A/B attendees from the app and matchmaking, invite with the same sequences, follow up afterwards. The 15-meeting target is realistic only if most are booked before 15 Nov.

### 5.5 Measurement
| Metric | What | Baseline |
|---|---|---|
| Invite acceptance | % LinkedIn invites accepted | set after first 100 |
| Reply rate | per sequence | 2% (previous outbound) |
| Meetings | booked calls with listed companies, incl. HLTH | target set with sales for Oct–Dec |
| Cost per meeting | time + budget (ads, tools) per meeting | from November |

## 10. Summary: five conclusions and action plan
1. **Purchase searches are few, but they exist.** 7 of 15 D1 purchase terms have demand ("medical device software development services" 480, KD 5; "healthcare ai consulting" 480, KD 12; "mlops consulting services" 390, KD 11). In Poland, 0 of 20.
2. **No organic or AI visibility.** Authority Score 6, no topical terms in the US top 20, 0 AI mentions. Educational topics are the path to authority and AI citations.
3. **The market is countable by name and regulation creates demand.** 1,614 FDA AI devices (335 new in 2025); USD 14.2bn digital health funding in 2025, 54% to AI. PCCP, AI guidance, cybersecurity and QMSR make MLOps and audit trails a regulatory requirement.
4. **LinkedIn audiences scale in the US, not in Poland.** D1: ~21,000 in the US; D2: 180,000 US vs 860 PL; D3: ~1,000. "MLOps for FDA after clearance" remains unclaimed.
5. **The site doesn't measure enquiries.** 11,260 sessions/year, 0 key events, ~90 real US sessions/month. Conversions and measurement come first.
